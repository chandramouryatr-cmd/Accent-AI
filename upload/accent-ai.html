MODULE 1 · DECONSTRUCTING THE SOUND
Understanding Vowels A–E
Reading Speed 120 WPM
Drag the slider to control how fast the text is highlighted word by word.
Your brain automatically maps new vowel sounds to the closest equivalent in your native language. We must disrupt this cognitive reflex before you speak a single word.

🧠 The Core Problem — Native Mapping: Before you record anything, your brain will default to a familiar phoneme. This lesson rewires that reflex through physical triggers and auditory contrast training.

TWO SOUNDS TO MASTER
/æ/ — "Cat"
The Vowel 'A'
Physical Trigger: Jaw Drop
Most non-native speakers don't drop their jaw low enough — resulting in "Eh" or "Ah". Lower your chin and feel the gap between your upper and lower teeth before recording.
/ε/ — "Bed"
The Vowel 'E'
Physical Trigger: Lip Spread
Learners often round or relax their lips too much. The target accent requires horizontal tension — a slight smile shape for phonetic resonance.
MODULE 1 · VISUALIZING THE FORMULA
The 3-Vector Formula
Every vowel is created by adjusting exactly three variables. The correct settings for /æ/ are pre-set below — explore how each one changes the sound.
↕
Jaw Position (Vertical)
Displays required mouth openness — High, Mid, or Low. For /æ/, the jaw drops to its lowest position.
↔
Lip Shape (Horizontal)
Rounded (O-shape) versus Unrounded (Smile-shape) tension. /æ/ requires an unrounded, spread shape.
≋
Tongue Posture
A cross-section view — tongue weight shifted Front, Central, or Back. For /æ/, the tongue sits Front-Low.
💡 Remember
These three parameters — Jaw, Lips, Tongue — fully determine every English vowel. Internalizing them converts an abstract sound into a reproducible physical action.
MODULE 1 · FIRST INTERACTION
The Hold & Match UI
Before saying a full word, hold a single isolated vowel for 3 seconds. This bypasses the "word reflex" that makes your brain revert to native habits.
3-Second Breath Meter

💡 Jaw Tip
Before pressing the button, physically lower your chin. Visualize the distance between your upper and lower teeth. The jaw drop is the key to /æ/ — don't rush into the sound.
